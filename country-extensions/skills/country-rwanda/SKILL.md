## 1. Regulatory Hierarchy

Rwanda operates a **centralised national framework administered through decentralised districts.** National codes are set centrally; building permits are issued by the **City of Kigali** or the relevant **District** via a one-stop centre.

| Authority | Scope | Key Instruments |
|---|---|---|
| **Ministry of Infrastructure (MININFRA)** | Construction policy, codes | National construction/housing policy |
| **Rwanda Housing Authority (RHA)** | Building code custodian, public buildings | **Rwanda Building Code**; **Building Control Regulations**; Green Building Minimum Compliance System |
| **City of Kigali / Districts** | Permits, zoning, master-plan enforcement | Building permits via **BPMIS**; **Kigali City Master Plan 2050** + district master plans |
| **Rwanda Standards Board (RSB)** | Product/material & technical standards | Rwanda Standards (adopt EAC/ISO/BS) |
| **REMA / RDB** | Environmental compliance | EIA / environmental clearance for larger projects |
| **RURA** | Utilities regulation | Regulates energy/water/telecom providers |
| **REG (EUCL / EDCL) & WASAC** | Electricity & water utilities | Connection approvals, energy access |

## 6. Structural, Geotechnical & Climate Overlays

- **Seismicity: significant in the west — do not treat Rwanda as aseismic.** Western Rwanda lies on the **East African Rift / Lake Kivu** zone (the 2008 Lake Kivu earthquake caused fatalities and damage around Rubavu/Gisenyi). Apply seismic design (e.g. EN 1998 / equivalent) with higher demand in the west; lower in the east. [VERIFY site seismic zone/PGA — western districts materially higher than Kigali/east.]
- **Terrain: "land of a thousand hills."** Steep slopes dominate — **slope stability, cut-and-fill, retaining structures, erosion control and stormwater management are first-order design issues.** Landslide risk in heavy rains; site grading and drainage are critical.
- **Climate: temperate tropical highland.** High altitude (Kigali ~1,500 m; higher in the north/west) gives mild temperatures despite the equatorial latitude — generally low cooling demand, natural ventilation often viable. **Two rainy seasons** with intense rainfall → robust roofing, drainage, and waterproofing; design for heavy storm events.
- **Materials:** strong policy push toward local/low-carbon materials; fired-clay brick, compressed stabilised earth blocks (CSEB), and locally produced cement are common. Confirm availability and the green-compliance materials expectations.

## 7. Approval Workflow (typical)

1. **Zoning / land use** — confirm against the **Kigali City Master Plan 2050** (or district master plan): land-use zone, plot coverage, FAR, height, setbacks.
2. **Building permit** — submit through **BPMIS** (Building Permit Management Information System) to the City of Kigali / District one-stop centre; architectural, structural and services review. Rwanda is known for relatively fast, digitised permitting.
3. **Environmental clearance** — **EIA via RDB/REMA** for larger or sensitive projects.
4. **Green Building Minimum Compliance** — demonstrate the mandatory minimum performance.
5. **Utilities** — **REG/EUCL** (power) and **WASAC** (water/sanitation) connections.
6. **Inspections & occupancy permit** — staged inspections, then occupancy certificate.

> Confirm professional registration requirements (engineers/architects via the Institute of Engineers Rwanda / professional councils) and contractor classification.
